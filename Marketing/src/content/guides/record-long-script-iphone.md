## Decide how the clips will become one video

A collection of good takes is not yet a finished video. Before filming, decide whether you will publish each section separately or assemble them into one piece.

Teleprompter Pal’s current workflow trims a selected beginning and end, crops the frame and exports that take. Plan to join separate clips in a multi-clip editor, such as iMovie. Do not assume the record button, prompt Restart control or export action will assemble them for you.

If your delivery requires one uninterrupted performance, separate takes will not meet that requirement. Otherwise, a section boundary lets you keep completed material when another part needs work.

## Cut at a change of thought, not at every sentence

Mark where the speaker finishes a promise, explanation or demonstration. Keep connected sentences together. A cut immediately before “because” or halfway through a comparison leaves the next clip dependent on an unfinished thought.

Read each section aloud. If you cannot finish it comfortably, simplify the wording or find an earlier complete idea. If the delivery sounds choppy, combine adjacent sentences rather than making every line a separate clip.

There is no universal clip length. For planning, 75 words at 150 words per minute is 30 seconds before pauses. The [speech-time calculator](/tools/speech-time-calculator/) can estimate a section, but time your own reading, including anything you show on camera.

When the same phrase keeps failing, use the [natural-reading exercise](/guides/read-teleprompter-naturally-iphone/) to work on wording and delivery before recording more attempts.

This is a fictional teaching script, not a recording test. Keep a note beside your library with each section ID, the actual clip you selected and its first and last spoken words. Use a filename, recording time or other identifier your recording destination actually shows. These example IDs are not automatically generated app labels.

A strong opening does not make an incomplete take usable. Play the whole selected section before marking it ready. There is no required number of attempts.

## Record and review one complete section

Keep the phone position, framing, lighting and microphone consistent across sections. If you move the chair or change the microphone distance, record another short sample and compare it with the previous section. If that sample is silent, [check the original recording before reshooting](/guides/teleprompter-video-no-sound-iphone/) so you can isolate the problem and preserve usable sections.

Before the first word, settle into your speaking position. Leave a little quiet space at both ends so you can remove the reach toward the phone without cutting into speech. Start the next section with similar energy, rather than restarting every clip at maximum enthusiasm.

If a section goes wrong, finish and save the recording deliberately, then make another attempt. Repeating a sentence within one recording can also work, but keeping good material on both sides of a bad middle passage requires a middle cut. A start/end trim alone cannot do that.

Check the recording indicator. Teleprompter Pal’s prompt controls use a speed you set; they do not follow your voice. Restarting the text does not stop an active camera recording. The [iPhone recording and export walkthrough](/guides/record-video-with-script-iphone/) covers those controls and saving a separate MP4.

## Join the selected clips, then check every boundary

For an iMovie assembly on iPhone, save the chosen clips to Photos first. Apple’s [instructions for combining videos](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102667) describe creating a Movie project, selecting clips and changing their order.

1. In iMovie, choose **Start New Project**, then **Movie**. Select your chosen clips and tap **Create Movie**.
2. Match the timeline to your section map. Rearrange clips if necessary; do not rely on recording order when you made retakes.
3. Trim unwanted material at the edges. Check the transition between clips. Apple documents **None** for a direct cut; the default is **Dissolve**.
4. Play the assembled video, then use **Done**, **Share** and **Save Video** when it is ready.

Listen across each join without watching. Check for repeated words, missing explanations, a sudden volume change or a pause that breaks the thought. Then watch for a jump in framing or an unfinished gesture. A transition effect cannot repair missing speech. Adjust the boundary or record the affected section again.

Finally, compare the saved result with S01, S02 and S03: each belongs once, and every promised instruction is present. Open that saved video with sound before deleting or replacing any source recording. These are suggested review steps; we have not performed a new device or iMovie test for this article.
