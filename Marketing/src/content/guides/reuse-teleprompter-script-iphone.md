## Build a master for the parts worth repeating

A master is a script you keep as a starting point. It might preserve the shape of a product demonstration or a recurring lesson. Keep details that change between videos out of the permanent wording.

For example, save this original demonstration skeleton as **Desk demo | MASTER**:

> Today I’m showing you [OBJECT].
>
> Start by checking [VISIBLE DETAIL].
>
> Then try [ONE ACTION].
>
> Before you finish, check [LIMIT OR EXCEPTION].
>
> Next, [ONE NEXT ACTION].

The brackets are editing prompts, not words to read aloud. Teleprompter Pal does not fill them in for you. Replace them in the working copy, then read the complete passage without scrolling. If it sounds stiff, use the [spoken-script rewrite example](/guides/read-teleprompter-naturally-iphone/#rewrite-the-sentence-you-would-never-say-aloud) to make the wording easier to deliver.

“MASTER” is a naming convention you apply yourself. It does not lock the entry or prevent an accidental edit.

## Duplicate the master before changing the subject

Teleprompter Pal’s current script editor has an editable **Script title** field and a **Duplicate** action in its script-actions menu.

1. Open the master script.
2. Open **Script actions**, the ellipsis menu, and choose **Duplicate**.
3. In the Scripts library, open the new entry. Its initial title adds “copy” to the original title. Confirm which entry you are editing before replacing any wording.
4. Change its title to something specific, such as **Desk demo | Notebook stand | v01**. Fill every bracketed field with the words for that video.
5. Tap **Done**, reopen the working entry and check the title and text you intended to keep.

Duplicate copies the title and words into a new script record. Editing the copy does not update the master, and a later improvement to the master does not flow into earlier copies. If several copies need the same correction, deliberately check and edit each affected entry.

You can also start another version from a working copy when the changes belong only to that subject. Duplicate the version you mean to revise, then rename the new entry before editing it.

The useful distinction is the changed instruction, not a larger pile of versions. If you decide to use v02, read its full passage to check that the new closing still follows the demonstration. Do not assume a higher version number means the wording has been reviewed.

After recording, note the script version beside the clip identifier you can actually find, such as its displayed recording time. Keep that note yourself; do not assume the example titles become video filenames or that the app creates this ledger.

## Check the words before pressing Record

Open the working script you intend to film and check its first line, the object or example in the middle, and the final action. Scan for any remaining bracketed prompts and for details carried over from the previous video.

For the notebook-stand example, this means checking both the named object and the revised folding instruction. Looking only at the title would miss an old closing pasted into a newly named entry.

Rehearse the complete passage once. Then follow the [iPhone recording walkthrough](/guides/record-video-with-script-iphone/) for the camera, sound check and saved take. A script revision and a video retake are different things: correcting text after filming does not replace speech already captured in the recording.

Keep the version you recorded until you have checked the video and no longer need that reference. If an improvement belongs in future videos too, make the change to the master deliberately, after preserving the working wording you need.

## Treat duplication and backup as separate jobs

Two entries in one app on one phone are still on the same device. A duplicate protects the master from your ordinary working edits; it is not an independent backup and does not establish cross-device synchronization.

For important scripts, keep a separate text copy in a location you maintain and can reopen. Check the beginning and end of that copy before relying on it. For device-level protection, use Apple’s [iPhone backup guidance](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/back-up-iphone-iph3ecf67d29/ios); a device backup is a separate workflow, not a promise of a per-script restore button in Teleprompter Pal.
