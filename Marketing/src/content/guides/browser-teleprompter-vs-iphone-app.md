A browser reader is a practical starting point when you already work at a computer. An iPhone app becomes useful when you want to keep the script, camera recording and editing workflow on the phone. Neither choice guarantees natural eye contact. Your screen position and a short recorded test will tell you more.

## Match the reader to the device doing the recording

| Your setup | A useful starting point | Check before committing |
|---|---|---|
| Laptop with a webcam | Browser teleprompter | Can the text sit close enough to the webcam? |
| iPhone recording while a laptop holds the script | Browser reader on the laptop | Does looking at the laptop pull your gaze away from the phone lens? |
| iPhone for the whole filming session | Native iPhone teleprompter app | Can you read, capture and handle retakes in the workflow you need? |
| Camera behind reflective teleprompter glass | Reader that fits your display and glass | Are the reflected words readable, correctly oriented and unclipped? |

The choice in the second row is easy to overlook. Using two devices can work, but you need to place them as one filming setup. A readable screen across the desk does not become a camera-facing script just because its text scrolls.

## Try the browser workflow with a real paragraph

Open the [free browser teleprompter](https://freeteleprompteronline.com/) and paste a paragraph from the video you plan to make. Set the text size from your filming position. Then adjust the column width until you can follow the lines without a large sideways sweep of your eyes.

Record a short test with your intended camera. Watch the saved clip at the size someone will actually view it. Look for moments when your gaze drops or moves sideways. If that happens, move the reading area closer to the lens before changing the script or buying another tool.

Keep the same paragraph while you adjust the setup. Changing the wording, camera position and scrolling pace at once makes it hard to know what helped.

For a reflective rig, use the [mirror setup guide](https://freeteleprompteronline.com/mirror-teleprompter/) and check the actual reflection. Mirrored text on the source screen can be exactly what the glass needs.

## Look at what happens after a mistake

The first clean sentence tells you less about a recording workflow than the first stumble. Test the recovery: stop, return to the start of the thought, and record it again. Then find the take you want and check how you would remove the unwanted beginning or change the framing.

If that work regularly sends you between a reader, a camera app and an editor, keeping those tasks together on your iPhone may be useful. If you already edit on a computer and the browser reader fits your camera position, staying with that setup may be the easier choice.

If you have settled on recording with the phone, [compare teleprompter apps for iPhone](/guides/best-teleprompter-apps-for-iphone/) by script limits, scrolling controls and the video you can export.

Write down the actual friction. “Finding my place after a retake” is a better reason to change tools than “I need something more professional.”
