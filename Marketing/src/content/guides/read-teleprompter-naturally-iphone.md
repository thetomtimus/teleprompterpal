## Find the lens before adjusting the script

Put the phone on a stable support and find the camera you are actually recording with. With a front-facing camera, your face appears on the screen, but the preview is not where the lens sits. Looking at your own image can pull your gaze away from the person watching.

Raise or lower the support so the lens is near your eye level. Stay in a position where you can read without leaning forward. Then bring the active reading area toward the lens, using the positioning controls your app provides. If the text covers too much of your face, adjust the prompt area rather than trying to watch yourself through it.

The text and lens still occupy different places. A phone app does not make that separation disappear. There is no font size or filming distance that works for every phone, eyesight and framing.

Production company Four Winds Creative also recommends checking eye line, rehearsing aloud and changing wording that trips you up. Our exercise below turns those broad checks into a repeatable phone-recording task. [Read its production guidance](https://fourwindscreative.com/on-camera-101-how-to-use-a-teleprompter-like-a-pro/).

## Compare two short takes, changing one thing

Use a few sentences from the script you actually plan to record. Keep the phone, light, microphone, wording and scroll pace the same.

Record take A with your current text position. For take B, move the reading area closer to the lens, if your app allows it. Watch both saved clips with the sound off, then listen with the sound on. This is a suggested practice exercise, not a test we have performed on your setup.

Make only the change relevant to the problem you see. A shorter line can help lateral reading, but a huge font can also leave too little upcoming text visible. Keep enough context to finish a phrase comfortably.

## Rewrite the sentence you would never say aloud

A script can be grammatically correct and still be awkward to deliver. Read it aloud once without scrolling. Wherever you have to restart, try saying the idea to one person in your own words.

Here is an original editing example.

**Written version**

> Before initiating the recording process, it is advisable to undertake a comprehensive assessment of your framing and audio configuration to ensure that the resulting footage satisfies the requirements of your intended distribution channel.

**Spoken version**

> Before you record, check your framing.
>
> Can you see everything you need in the shot?
>
> Does the shape fit where you will post it?
>
> Now record a short sample.
>
> Play it back and listen to the sound.

The rewrite names actions instead of asking you to carry one long sentence. The line breaks show places to finish a thought; you do not have to pause after every line. Keep technical or approved wording when accuracy requires it, and simplify the sentences around it.

Use emphasis where the meaning needs it. You do not need a gesture or a change of tone on every sentence.

## Leave room for the pauses you actually take

Start the scrolling prompt and speak at a comfortable pace. Include the breaths, emphasis and pauses you intend to use in the final take. If you rush to catch a disappearing line, slow the scrolling or shorten that passage. If you keep waiting for the next thought, adjust the pace and try again.

A duration estimate can help you plan the script. The [speech-time calculator](/tools/speech-time-calculator/) includes an allowance for pauses, but words per minute is not the same as an app's scroll-speed setting.

With set-speed scrolling, the text continues moving while you stop speaking unless you pause it. Teleprompter Pal's current beta uses manual scrolling controls; it does not listen and wait for your voice. If speech following is a requirement, [compare the iPhone app options](/guides/best-teleprompter-apps-for-iphone/) before choosing.

Rehearse the awkward sentence again after changing it. A faster scroll cannot fix wording you struggle to say.

## Restart a complete thought when you stumble

Suppose your intended sentence is:

> Save the original before you crop the video.

You say, “Save the original before you… before the crop.” Stop speaking. If the text keeps moving, pause the reader. Take a breath, find the beginning of the sentence and say the complete line again. Leave a little quiet space around the restart so you can locate it during review.

Then choose the edit the clip needs. If the clean version is at the end and you no longer need the opening, trimming the start may be enough. If you must keep material both before and after a mistake in the middle, you need a middle cut or separate clips, not just a start/end trim. Apple's Photos instructions describe [trimming a video's beginning and end](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/trim-video-length-adjust-speed-and-edit-audio-iph1d1344a2d/ios).

Pal's current editor trims a selected start/end range. Do not plan a recording around an automatic removal of every mistake.

## Review the saved clip before the full script

Watch the sample at the size someone will view it. Check whether your gaze stays near the camera, whether the words sound like something you would say, and whether the pauses give the listener room to follow.

Listen for missing words, clipped starts and background sound. Play an exported sample too if exporting is part of your workflow. A readable prompt is useful only if the resulting video works.

Keep the version that improved the specific problem. Use that setup for the next section of your script, then check again if you change the phone orientation or filming position.
